About Naval Pursuit

The classic naval duel, rebuilt for the whole crew.

The classic naval guessing game is played by two people. Naval Pursuit puts up to eleven fleets in the same water, so every shot could hit anyone, and the real game is working out who's hiding where.

Why it exists

Game night shouldn't need a sign-up form.

"I made this app to be as local as I could so it would be as easy as clicking a link to play."

Naval Pursuit is an independent game, designed and built by one developer for playing with friends and family. The goal from the start was a game a group could start in seconds: send a four-letter code or a link, everyone picks a callsign, and you're deploying ships.

Putting every fleet on one board changed the game. Hits don't light up on the grid. Instead, each square shows the round it was hit in, and each commander's tracker shows the rounds their ships were hit. Line those up and you can work out where someone's fleet is hiding. It's part battle and part puzzle, and alliances and betrayals happen naturally.

It also had to fit how groups actually play. Not everyone is free at the same moment, so a battle can run all day: take your turn when you can, and a notification lets you know when it's your move again.

Principles

What Naval Pursuit stands for

01

Zero friction

No accounts, no passwords and no download required. It runs in any modern browser, installs to your home screen, and has a native Android app.

02

Respect for players

No ads, no in-app purchases, and nothing about you is sold. Sectors are deleted when the battle ends. The privacy policy spells out the rest.

03

Friendly by design

Quick comms are ready-made lines and emoji, so trash talk stays fun and there's nothing to moderate. Callsigns can be anything you like.
