Privacy Policy

Your data, briefed plainly.

Naval Pursuit is built to need as little about you as possible: no accounts, no ads, and sectors are deleted from the server when the battle ends. Here's exactly what it does handle.

02Who this covers

This policy covers the Naval Pursuit web app at game.navalpursuit.com (including when it's installed to your home screen), the Naval Pursuit Android app, the game servers we run, and this website. Together we call them "Naval Pursuit" or "the App". Naval Pursuit is made by an independent developer, referred to here as "we" or "us".

Servers run by other people. The Android app can connect to any Naval Pursuit server you add by address or invite link. If you play on a server someone else runs, that server's operator, not us, controls the game data and logs on it.

03What we collect

When you create or join a sector, your device sends the game server what it needs to run the battle:

DataWhat it isWhy
CallsignThe name you choose when you create or join a sector.Shown to the other players in that sector. Use a nickname: it doesn't need to be your real name.
GameplayYour grid size choice, ship placements, shots, ready signals, quick comms lines and emoji, and the stats the game keeps (shots, hits, ships sunk).To run the game and show the results to everyone in the sector.
Seat credentialsA random player ID and secret the server issues when you join.So you can reconnect to your seat if you close the app or lose signal. They aren't linked to who you are.
Notification addressOnly if you turn notifications on: a Firebase registration token (web) or Firebase Installation ID (Android).To tell you when it's your turn and about other sector events. See Notifications.
IP addressThe network address your connection comes from.Held in memory to limit requests and stop abuse. See below.

Server logs

Our game server writes operational logs: server start-up and errors, and a short line for game events, such as a sector being created or closed and in-game events like "MAVERICK hit GOOSE's Frigate", which include callsigns and the sector code. At the normal logging level, these logs don't include IP addresses. The server runs as a container under systemd, so logs go to the host's system journal, which rotates old entries out automatically. Like any website, the network and hosting in front of the server may briefly record connection details such as IP addresses and timestamps for security and diagnostics.

Backups

So a game in progress survives a server restart, the server saves running sectors to disk about once a minute. The backup is encrypted (AES-256-GCM) and holds only sectors that still exist: when a sector is deleted, it drops out of the next backup.

What we don't collect

We don't ask for or collect your name, email, phone number, contacts, photos, precise location, payment details or free-text messages. There are no in-app purchases and no ads.

04Stored on your device

Some things are kept only on your own phone or computer (in the browser's local storage for the web app, or the app's private storage on Android) and are never sent to our servers unless noted:

  • Your callsign, if you leave "save name" on, so you don't have to type it each time.
  • Your settings, such as haptics, quick comms muting, hit highlights, notification preferences, quiet hours and muted sectors.
  • Your service record: lifetime stats such as games played and won. These stay on your device.
  • Your current sectors: the sector code, server address and seat credentials, so you can rejoin. The Android app also keeps results of finished games on your list until you dismiss them.
  • Whether you've seen the guided tour, the latest changelog and this policy.

Transfers. If you choose to move your seat or service record to another device, the app makes a code or link holding your callsign, stats and, for a seat, its credentials. It's only shared where you send it, so treat it like a password.

Android backup. If Android backup is on, your settings and service record may be included in your device's Google backup, so they come back on a new phone. Seat credentials are excluded.

05Notifications

Notifications are optional. They let a battle run all day: we'll tell you when it's your turn, when everyone's ready or combat begins, when your fleet is sunk, remind you about an untaken turn, and warn you before a sector closes.

  • How they work. We use Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM). When you allow notifications, Firebase gives your browser or app an address (a registration token on the web, a Firebase Installation ID on Android), which your device sends to the game server with your seat. The server sends that address the notification (the sector code and what happened) through Firebase.
  • Web app. Nothing is requested until you turn notifications on and your browser asks for permission. To deliver notifications, the app's service worker loads Firebase and Workbox scripts from Google's servers (gstatic.com and storage.googleapis.com).
  • Android app. Android 13 and later asks for your permission first. You can turn notifications off in the app's settings, set quiet hours, mute a sector, or choose which kinds you get in Android's settings.
  • Deletion. The server stores the notification address with your seat in that sector only, and deletes it when the sector is deleted or the address stops working. Undelivered notifications are dropped after 24 hours.

06Analytics

The web app has no analytics and no tracking cookies.

The Android app includes Google Analytics for Firebase, to help us see how many people play, which devices and Android versions to support, and how the app is used. Firebase collects this automatically, and it can include an app instance ID, your device model, Android version, app version, language, an approximate location (country or region, worked out from your IP address), and events such as the first time you open the app and how long sessions last. On Android it may also read the device's advertising ID, which you can reset or delete in Android's settings. We look at this in aggregate. We don't use it to identify you, don't combine it with your callsign or game data, and don't use it for advertising.

Google processes this data under its own terms. See Google's Privacy Policy and Privacy and Security in Firebase.

07Who sees what

  • Other players in your sector see your callsign, whether you're ready and connected, your quick comms, your shots, the round numbers where shots landed, damage to your fleet and your stats, as the rules of the game show them. They never see where your unhit ships are.
  • Service providers. Google (Firebase) delivers notifications and, in the Android app, provides analytics. This website is hosted on GitHub Pages, which may log visitors' IP addresses for security; see the GitHub Privacy Statement.
  • Server administrator. The server's admin console, which only the operator can reach, shows active sectors and server stats so problems can be fixed.
  • The law. We'd disclose information only if legally required to, and given how little we keep, there'd rarely be anything to disclose.

We don't sell or rent personal information, and we don't share it for advertising.

08How long it's kept

We don't keep a database of players, accounts or past games. Everything about a sector lives on the server only while that sector exists, and the server deletes it automatically:

Sector stateDeleted after
Created, nobody else joined30 minutes
Deploying ships12 hours
In combat24 hours with no shot fired
Game over10 minutes after the last shot

A host can also delete their sector at any time. When a sector is deleted, its callsigns, fleets, shots, logs, seat credentials and notification addresses go with it. Server log lines rotate out of the system journal on the host's normal schedule. IP addresses used for rate limiting are kept in memory only and expire within minutes. Data stored on your device stays until you clear it. Firebase keeps analytics data according to Google's retention settings.

09Permissions

Android app

  • Internet, to connect to game servers.
  • Vibrate, for haptic feedback such as your volley landing or your fleet being hit. You can turn haptics off in Settings.
  • Notifications, to tell you about your turn and sector events while the app is closed. This is optional.

Web app

  • Notifications, only if you turn them on.
  • Keep screen awake, so your screen doesn't dim mid-battle. No data is involved.
  • Clipboard, only when you tap to copy an invite link or transfer code.

10Your choices

  • Play without notifications. Leave them off, or turn them off at any time in Settings or your device's settings.
  • Leave a sector to stop taking part. Your seat's data is deleted with the sector.
  • Clear what's on your device by clearing the site's data in your browser, or the app's storage in Android's settings, or by uninstalling the app.
  • Limit analytics on Android by resetting or deleting your advertising ID in Android's settings. Uninstalling the app stops analytics entirely.
  • Ask us. Because there are no accounts, we usually can't tell which data is yours, and sectors are deleted on their own. If you have a request about your data, including under laws like the GDPR or CCPA, contact us with the sector code and callsign and we'll help.

11Security

Our servers use encrypted connections (HTTPS and secure WebSockets), the game server runs in a locked-down container, backups are encrypted, and the admin console isn't exposed to the internet. Seat credentials are random and unrelated to who you are. No system is perfectly secure, but there's very little to protect in the first place.

12Children

Naval Pursuit isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them. Players never need to give a real name, and chat is limited to ready-made lines. If you believe a child has given us personal information, contact us and we'll delete it.

13Changes to this policy

If what the App collects changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top. For significant changes, we'll also note them in the app's "What's New".

14Contact

Questions or requests about this policy or your data? Open an issue on the Naval Pursuit site's GitHub page and we'll get back to you.
