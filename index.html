How it works

Deploy. Fire. Deduce.

Here, everyone shares the same water, so every shot fired might hit anyone, including you.

01

Deploy

Place your five ships horizontally, vertically or diagonally, or let the game auto-configure them for you. Mark your ships ready, and the host starts the battle.

02

Fire

You get one shot per unsunk ship. You lose ships, you lose firepower. Mark targets during other players' turns so your volley is ready the moment yours begins.

03

Deduce

Each player's tracker shows the rounds their ships were hit. Cross-referance the trackers with the board to reveal your opponents' position, but be vigilent they may be on top of you.

FAQ

Before you set sail

Do I need to download anything?

No. Naval Pursuit runs in any modern browser on your phone, tablet or computer. You'll get the best experience on mobile browsers by installing it as a PWA from the main menu.

Do I need an account?

No. I made this app to be as local as I could so it would be easy as clicking a link to play.

How many people can play?

2 to 11 commanders per sector. I recommend up to 6 in a 10x10, 8 in a 13X13, and 11 in 15X15.

How do I invite friends?

Create a sector and send your friends its four-letter code or the invite link. They join straight from their own device.

Do we have to play at the same time?

Nope, this game is designed to be as flexible and convienent as possible. Sectors last up to 24hrs between turns and notifications make sure that you don't forget about your game.

Is there a mobile app?

An Android app is in early access testing, and you can sign up to join the testers. Naval Pursuit can also be installed as a Progressive Web App (PWA) on Google Chrome and Safari.

About

Made for game night

My in-laws started it. Back in 2025, we started playing the paper version of this game together and it quickly be came a family favorite. However there were two recurring issues: shots had to be tracked manually and playing remotely was challenging, especially with games lasting sometimes an hour or more. So I decided to come up with a digital version that would serve both of those needs. I started December 2025 and finished the first version in February 2026. That version remained the same for most of the year outside of regular dependancy updates. This version was created out of the learning from the previous few months playing it.

A quick note on how this game was built: I’m an IT admin with some Python experience, not a full-time software engineer. For this project, I used AI assistants (Jules, Gemini, and Claude) to do the heavy lifting with the code. My role was making the high-level decisions, reviewing the AI's work, and most importantly applying my IT background to harden the infrastructure and keep the game secure by design. I have put a lot of time and hours into it and I think it's turned out great.

If you've made it this far, thanks for your time and attention, and I hope you enjoy playing the game with your friends and family; that's truly why I built this.

— Matthew Sandau
